Why it fails now

Hyderabad peaks move off the clock. TL-2362 peak MAE is 1.13 kW. The same hour last week is 0.26 kW. Yesterday-copy is 0.52 kW, so the issued desk stays yesterday-copy. Load runs from about 0.2 kW overnight to spikes near 7 kW, and those spikes shift by hours. The noon climatology is real (train peak 4.09 kW at 12:00). City temperature does not see a geyser or an air conditioner switching.

The spike check lost on the holdout. A two-part model estimates the chance load exceeds 2 kW, and the size of that spike, with the quiet-hour curve underneath. At the validation cutoff of 0.25 it recalls 80% of hours above 2 kW, at 60% precision. The placement that won the validation week, a spike where that probability exceeds 0.70, is 60.6% wMAPE and 1.03 kW peak MAE on the holdout. The TiDE point is 54.8% and 1.13 kW. Average error got worse, and the peak still loses to yesterday, so the point stays the TiDE.
